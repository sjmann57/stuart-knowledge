## 2. LinkedIn Post (Stuart's version — published)

21 days of daily posting. Here is what I have learned, and what comes next.

Three weeks ago, I set myself a challenge. Post every day on LinkedIn. No excuses, no skipped days, no "I'll do two tomorrow." It sounded straightforward. It was not.

Day 10, I wrote about Ironman Lanzarote and the daily habits that get you to a finish line. The same principle applies here. It is not one big post that builds a presence. It is the consistency, the showing up when you do not feel like it, the quick coffee-and-keyboard before the day starts.

What surprised me most was the response. Security architecture, identity controls, Sentinel, Defender, and Secure Score, topics I live and breathe every day, resonated with people dealing with the same problems. The gap between having a Microsoft security and having a working security posture is not unique to any one organisation. It is everywhere.

I have also written about Ironman, the Water Orton Carnival, the Eisenhower Matrix, and Sunday mornings with a coffee. Because security architects are also human beings, and a LinkedIn presence built entirely on technical posts is a profile, not a person.

So what comes next?

The next 79 days of my challenge are to get me to 100 posts over 100 days. More on identity, cloud security, security operations, and the real-world gap between what organisations have configured and what they actually need. And more of the human side, as this is a social platform, and I like to be sociable.

If any of the topics I have covered have resonated, connect with me and follow along. There is more to come.
