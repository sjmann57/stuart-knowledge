## LinkedIn Post (published — 2026-09-05)

Attitude

Attitude is a word that can bring out a few instant reactions. What was yours?

Was it negative or positive when you read the word?

We hear the word used in so many different ways, and depending on the tone and situation, it can have very different connotations.

* "You've got an attitude."
* "Change your attitude."
* "That's the right attitude."

Same word. Very different meaning.

For me, I tend to see the glass as half full, as they say.

I always look for the positive in how I tackle each day, the challenges in front of me, and the things I want to achieve.

But there is a but.

My attitude might be fired up, but sometimes my inner confidence needs a bit of a shake.

If you know me, you probably see someone who can be loud, full of energy and appear to have all the confidence in the world.

What you might not see is the conversation going on inside. That is a daily internal fight. One I have become much better at managing over the years.

And that word, attitude, has a lot to do with it.

My attitude is often:

* Say yes, even when inside I am screaming no.
* Step forward anyway.

Not because I am always confident; sometimes it is quite the opposite.

But because I have learned that confidence doesn't always have to come first. Sometimes you have to take the step first and let the confidence catch up. Yes, that is sometimes scary.

Excited and scared at the same time

In a few days, I am stepping forward again.

I am going back to public speaking on a webinar, and it is a bit of a double-edged sword. Genuinely, I am excited about it, but I am also nervous. I know the subject. I know what I want to talk about

But that little voice is still there.

* What if you forget what you wanted to say?
* What if it doesn't go to plan?
* What if you get something wrong?

Then attitude kicks in.

Because I also know what will probably happen.

* I will start talking.
* The nerves will settle.
* The words will come.

It is the same when I stand in front of 60+ people in Scouting, talking not just to the young people but to a room full of adults and visitors.

* I might not say exactly what I planned.
* I might go off topic a little.
* I might remember something halfway through and add it in.

But the message will still get out, and that is what matters.

Maybe confidence doesn't have to come first

We often talk about needing more confidence before doing something. I am not sure that is always the answer. Maybe sometimes you need the right attitude first.

* The attitude to look for the light rather than the dark.
* To look for what could go right rather than everything that could go wrong.
* To tell yourself: I can do this.

Not because you know everything will go perfectly, but because you know that if it doesn't, you will deal with it.

For me, that is positive mental attitude. It isn't pretending everything is brilliant. It isn't ignoring nerves, doubt or fear. It is acknowledging they are there and choosing to step forward anyway.

So, in a few days, that is exactly what I will do.

* Stand tall.
* Take a deep breath.
* Probably have that last-minute conversation with myself.

Think about my attitude, and step forward. Because experience tells me something else too.

I will be all right on the night.

And once I start, I will probably wonder what I was worried about in the first place.

What do you tell yourself when confidence disappears but you know you still need to step forward?
