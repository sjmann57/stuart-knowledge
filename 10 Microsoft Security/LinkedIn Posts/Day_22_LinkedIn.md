## LinkedIn Post (Stuart's version — published)

I am excited to say I have left Trustmaque Ultima...

One of my shortest permanent roles by quite a way, and something I was not expecting. When the merger occurred, as these things do, it brought changes that affected not just the company's structure but also daily work. Teams were reorganised, responsibilities shifted, and I found myself moving into a different team than the one I originally joined. The past 9 months have taught me a few new lessons and reinforced what I already look for in teams and organisations I work for and with.

My experiences and takeaways continue to shape my perspective on what I seek in my future roles and teams. The key for me has always been a strong moral compass, with a focus on culture, values, honesty, and integrity, something I will again be seeking in my next role.

All of this has made me reflect on what, truly, good looks like, the experiences I have worked with many fabulous organisations, and what I recommend others consider.

My working career started with one of the toughest and kindest people I have ever called boss, Mr Norman Shakes. He was a self-starter, a business owner, taking a gamble on a 17-year-old on a training scheme. Norman ran a typewriter sales, repair and office supply company, and my journey in the service industry started right there. He taught me everything about the business, accounts, payroll, tea maker, shop assistant, typewriter mechanic, computer installations, and, most importantly, how to approach problem-solving quickly and efficiently. Norman also allowed me to make mistakes and guided me to fix what went wrong. I have many stories I could tell about these days. He sadly passed away a number of years ago, and I had the honour of going to his funeral. I found out how much he valued community and helped so many other people over his lifetime, a true gentleman and a great honour to have known him.

Next, let's talk about teamwork, social connection, and my time at Professional Computer Solutions. It was a busy and demanding place to work; we worked all hours, early morning, late nights, and long days. I was there to install and deploy computer systems, just as the Internet was really starting and before Google was even a thing. I worked alongside some great people, including Steve Teasdale, David Walsh, and many others; the list could go on and on. The sense of community at that time felt unmatched; we all worked in the office, were of a similar age, and worked and socialised as a collective. It was a time when my life underwent many changes and when I met my best friend, Jeanie, who is now my wife. It was also where I found that I could help and mentor those around me, passing on small tips and tricks to help others get more from themselves and grow.

The next team to talk about was at Foseco, a move away from the external clients, working in-house in a security and central IT role for the global organisation. My interview for that role was epic. I spent the day with Roger Chadderton and Cath Robbison going through all the interview stages, and a citametiric testing, in one day. It was so busy that it didn't even stop for lunch.

Thanks to both Cath and Roger, I really grew; they invested in sharpening my soft skills and in supporting me to grow not only as a technical person but as a human. I learnt so much in my time with both of them, including time management, self-awareness, dealing with people across the globe, and running global projects from a people perspective. The core of the team was teamwork: supporting each other and getting the best out of yourself and those around you. A team that supports you in more than just technical skills is a rare and, I would say, critical thing to find as early in your career as possible.

"Never stop learning from those around you" was the key to that team, to the point where I had taken over much of what Cath was responsible for, and we had achieved our collective goals.

Some of the people I have worked alongside and become good friends with along the way are a testament to the importance of picking the right teams. I have so many that I could mention that if I were ever in a jam and needed some help and advice, I could call them amazing. Some of them I have called upon more recently, such as Uri, Alastair, Jose, Marcus, Sam, Kian, and Alan.

Something I found very strange when I joined Performanta was that they talked about the Purple Tribe, but the longer I was there, the more sense it made to me. I have always found my tribe, my people, my friends in any organisation I am part of, which helps me become the most successful. I have been looking for my new tribe, and I will share that with you soon enough. In my last role, I didn't find my tribe after the re-organisation, and that is what I have learnt: if the structure doesn't align with your moral compass, values, and expectations, then that is not your tribe. It may suit others, but not every tribe is your tribe. If moving on is right for you, then it's right for you.

People often ask how to find their tribe or team in a new company. Here are a few practical tips that have helped me along the way:

* Spend time with your future team if you can, even informally. Try to meet them outside the formal interview process, such as over a coffee or lunch, to get a real sense of how they interact and support each other.
* Ask about the team's ways of working and what their typical day looks like. Listen for signs of collaboration, openness and support, or if everyone seems to work in silos.
* Speak with people at different levels in the team, not just your future manager. Hearing different perspectives can reveal what the real culture is like on a day-to-day basis.
* Trust your gut during every conversation. If you feel you can be yourself and are comfortable sharing ideas or asking questions, it's a good indicator that you might belong there.
* Look out for shared values. Do the team's actions and attitudes reflect what matters to you most, whether that's honesty, kindness, creativity, or ambition?
* Don't be afraid to ask current or former team members about their experiences, highs and lows. People are usually happy to share what they enjoy and what they think could be better.

By taking these steps, you'll be better placed to figure out if a team feels like home before you commit. The right fit is out there for everyone; sometimes you just need to be intentional and curious as you search for your tribe.

So, next time you are thinking about a new role, my considerations are:

* What is the culture like, and who will support and manage you? Are they your tribe?
* Have you had the chance to speak to others who work there, or do you know people in your network you can speak with?
* Also, make sure you are yourself in the interviews. When you walk through the door on the first day, you will be you. It has helped me find where I am not suited and where I am not.
* If you need to push back on anything, and I suggest that if you can, to do that, how the new organisation handles that will tell you a lot about them. As I said to Cath and Roger, I am just as fussy as you are about who works for you, as I am about who I go to work for. I still remember their faces.
* Make sure you are fussy, ask all the questions you need, and take a moment to imagine working with the team you are joining; you will be spending a lot of time with them.
