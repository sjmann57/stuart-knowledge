## LinkedIn Post (Stuart's version — published 2026-07-20)

Take a breath…

Mental wellbeing is talked about more than it used to be. Organisations have wellbeing apps, with champions and charities, and people are becoming more open about their experiences.

But when you need help, where do you turn?

Many of us will face physical or mental health challenges. I know that, as a man, I am statistically less likely to ask for help. Talking to family, friends or a professional has not always come naturally.

I've had my own challenges. My coping mechanisms include sport, exercise and Scouting. They give me purpose, routine and time to think.

But sometimes they haven't been enough.

Asking for more help felt difficult. Recognising that I needed help was probably the hardest part. I have been fortunate to have family and friends who noticed when I wasn't myself and encouraged me to take the next step.

The conversation needs to start and continue. We need to be more open, reduce the stigma and accept that it's OK to talk about how we feel.

If you find yourself struggling, this would be my advice.

* Be kind to yourself. It sounds simple, but it matters.
* Talk to someone you trust who will listen without judging.
* Speak to your GP. Mine listened, discussed the options and referred me for talking therapy.
* Try a recommended wellbeing app. I was referred to an AI-based chatbot that helped alongside other support.
* If you can't speak to someone close to you, use a helpline. They are there when you feel alone.

You don't have to face everything on your own.

Asking for help isn't a sign of weakness. Sometimes it's the strongest decision you can make.

If this post encourages one person to reach out, it was worth writing.

If you need support, these organisations are there to help.

Samaritans
Call 116 123 free, 24 hours a day.
https://www.samaritans.org

NHS 111
For urgent mental health support, call 111 and select the mental health option where available.

Mind

https://www.mind.org.uk

Shout

Text SHOUT to 85258
https://giveusashout.org

CALM
Support for anyone struggling or in crisis.
Call 0800 58 58 58

SANEline
Emotional support and practical information.
Call 0300 304 7000
https://www.sane.org.uk

PAPYRUS HOPELINE247
Suicide prevention support for under-35s and anyone worried about a young person.
Call 0800 068 4141
Text 88247
https://www.papyrus-uk.org

If someone is in immediate danger, or you're worried about your own safety, call 999 or go to your nearest A&E department.

You are not alone, and asking for help is one of the strongest things you can do.

There is always someone there to help. Please, if you want to talk, reach out and start a conversation.
