## LinkedIn Post (Stuart's version — published 2026-07-26)

A post this week reminded me of a principle that has shaped much of my career.

A post from José Lázaro prompted me to reflect on it. His commitment to supporting and empowering people is something I genuinely admire.

Whenever I've interviewed for a role, or considered a new opportunity, my thought process has always been:

• What will I be doing?
• Does the role interest me?
• What is the culture?
• Who will be managing and supporting me?

That last question has always been one of my highest priorities.

Why?

Because you work closely with your manager through successes, setbacks, pressure and change. You need confidence that they can handle all of that without damaging trust, relationships or the wider team.

They also need to challenge you.

The best managers I've worked for have stretched me beyond what I thought I was capable of. They've supported me, challenged me, given honest feedback and helped me grow.

That is a high bar.

It should be.

The relationship works both ways.

In my career, only one manager failed to meet that standard, and that only happened after an organisational restructure moved me into a different team. It reinforced why I place so much importance on choosing the right people to work with.

Every great manager I've had has led differently, different styles and approaches.

What they all had in common was how they treated people.

We spend a huge part of our lives at work. It should be somewhere you enjoy being.

The same principle applies when you run your own business. You may not have a traditional manager, but you still answer to clients, investors, shareholders or a board. Those relationships deserve the same level of trust and respect.

I'm fortunate to have worked with some outstanding leaders, including:

Norman Shakes, Steve Teasdale, Cath Robinson, Roger Chadderton, Alastair Cox, José Lázaro, Marcus Burnap & now Luke Elston

Thank you for the opportunities, support and challenges you've given me over the years.

Who would be on your list?

What's your non-negotiable when choosing where you work?
