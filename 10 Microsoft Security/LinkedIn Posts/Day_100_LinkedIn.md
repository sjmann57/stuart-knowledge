## LinkedIn Article (published — 2026-09-12)

100 posts in 100 days: DONE

Today I am writing the final post of the challenge.

I must admit, I am a bit torn, excited that I have completed the challenge and reached my goal, but sad that it's over. Ok, that might be a little strong.

Looking back, at 20 days in, I wondered if I would make it to the full 100.

When I posted that I was going to post on LinkedIn 100 times in 100 days, it meant I had nowhere to hide, and I like that about myself.

What 100 days gave me

As with races like Ironman, the debrief at the end and sharing my experiences is a great way to bring things to a close.

So, let's get into the past 100 days

It started with being out of work after some shenanigans at my previous company, and a need to build my own personal brand. At first, I watched Peter do his 100 posts in 100 days, and I was inspired.

New Role

Early in the challenge, I had great conversations with several organisations, which restored my faith in myself and my skills. When life throws twists you cannot control and knocks you off course, it has a way of denting self-confidence and belief.

I found an organisation that brings out the best in me, and although I am early in this journey, I hope I am adding value, making a difference, and enjoying what I do.

I joined CyberOne as a Senior Microsoft 365 Security Consultant, found my mojo, and do what I enjoy: working in a team, sharing a common goal, and helping clients achieve their security goals. This past week has been great, supporting the team in client meetings and AI webinar.

A chance to educate

What I wanted, but was surprised about when it happened. I originally thought I would get challenged by my writing, the imposter syndrome, I guess. What actually happened was that I was asked for advice, not so much on the technology, but on how to learn more, career advice, and genuine questions about learning more.

Being able to give back to others, at different phases of their learning and careers, has been a privilege. Although I cannot tell them what to do or how to learn, I hope I challenged them to look at the next steps more clearly. I wish everyone I have helped the very best.

Also, I educated myself; I had to learn, read, and do some labs myself to put articles together. I would have liked to go deeper into some areas, but time didn't allow it over the past 100 days. I will be going back, still more to learn,

I learned a lot writing them, and I hope you learned something reading them too.

Who Am I?

The writing was not all about tech; it was also about who I am.

What I realised at the beginning: my friends and work colleagues know my value, but others didn't, so it was time to share who I am with the world.

I have talked about how I used the skills from my career in my personal life and vice versa. They are entwined and benefit each other.

So I covered

* Scouting
* Endurance racing with Triathlons, with a focus on Ironman.
* Mental health and well-being.
* Mindset: the good and the not-so-good.
* When things go wrong.
* Also, what happened on that specific day.

People connect with people, and although fewer were interested in my more personal posts, that does not make them less valuable, and maybe that is a missed opportunity.

If I wrote 100 technical articles, that would have been great, but it would have been a workload that negatively impacted my life, so my life was the rest bite and additional educational path for those who read my articles.

What Next?

When I started posting, I began getting interesting comments from friends who saw me show up consistently in their feeds. They kept asking me the same question: was I looking to become a Microsoft Most Valuable Professional (MVP)?

Well, the answer initially was No.

The more I was asked and thought about it, the more I realised it was a good decision, but I needed to do more than the articles I'd already written. So, I am doing more webinars, talking about Microsoft security with CyberOne.

I will need to do more, and that is my next challenge to work on.

Will I continue to create content?

Yes, I will be, but at a lower cadence.

My mix of content and insight will continue, from technical to personal. I may have more in-depth technical how-tos, may even get to creating videos, or even YouTube or TikTok; maybe I am getting too carried away.

It will always end up one way or another on LinkedIn

Thank You

If you found what I created interesting, smiled when I got on the wrong train from London, or found something motivating, thank you for being part of the challenge.

I enjoyed the process, even if it ended up being just me, but, it wasn't; others joined me.

No questions to close out

No call to action

Just a simple

Thank You
