## LinkedIn Post (published — 2026-08-01)

Positive mental attitude.

It's a phrase we hear a lot, but I don't think it means being positive all the time.

For me, it means looking forward. Having faith in yourself. Seeing what is possible, even when you're carrying self-doubt.

That mindset has helped me in many parts of my life.

Training for my first Ironman meant believing I could swim, cycle and run further than I'd ever done before... all in one day.

Every time I've moved to a new role, I've needed the confidence to believe I could make a difference, bring my own character and help build something bigger than myself.

In Scouting, it's about seeing potential before it becomes reality. Helping young people and adult volunteers grow beyond the limits they thought they had.

That doesn't mean my mindset is always positive.

Like everyone, I've had times when I've felt overwhelmed.

What I've learnt is that mindset isn't one decision. It's something you build every day.

For me that includes:
• Exercising each morning.
• Listening to audiobooks that help me learn from other people's experiences.
• Taking a walk when I need to reset.
• Spending time with people who lift me up rather than pull me down.

The people around you matter more than many of us realise.

I've also learnt that asking for help is a strength, not a weakness. Sometimes that's a conversation with a friend. Sometimes it's speaking to a professional or your GP. I've needed support myself, and I'm glad I asked for it.

So today I'd leave you with one question.

How would you rate your mindset today?

Take a few minutes to check in with yourself. Think about the good things you already have: your family, partner, friends, pets, your health, or simply something that made you smile today.

Sometimes changing your perspective is the first step towards changing your direction.
