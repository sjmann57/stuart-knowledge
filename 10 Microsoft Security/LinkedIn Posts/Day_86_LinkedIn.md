## LinkedIn Post (published — 2026-08-29)

Positive Mental Attitude is easy to talk about when everything is going well.

But what about when it isn't?

I've had a year that at times has felt like being on the sidelines, following what, looking back, was a poor choice around 12 months ago. Not that every decision 12 months ago was wrong, but it has impacted good decisions too.

But that year has also given me a new perspective.

It has taught me a lot and reinforced some really important cornerstones of who I have worked to become.

When you are doubted, and people question who you are and what you can offer, eventually your mind starts asking those same questions.

* Am I good enough?
* Have I got this wrong?
* Maybe they are right?

Even with those doubts, I have kept following key principles I have been building since my early 20's.

The first is learning.

Reading, or now in my case listening to books, learning from the stories and lessons of others and finding ways I can improve myself.

That improvement doesn't happen overnight.

Mine hasn't happened over weeks or months. It has happened over years, and it is still happening.

The second is people.

The real key building blocks are the people around me and who I associate with.

Looking back over the last year, some of the people I associated with through my previous employment, particularly early in the year, were not the right people for me to be around.

But I am lucky.

I have people around me who lift me.

People who see what I can be, rather than where I might be standing on the days when my self-belief has taken a knock.

Building a Positive Mental Attitude isn't about always moving forward.

That isn't how life works.

Being positive doesn't always come naturally either.

We can easily look at the glass as half empty, especially when challenges cloud our thoughts.

I've been getting some guidance on this recently as part of my own development.

Finding ways to calm those thoughts, especially late at night or when you wake up with your mind already racing, can be difficult.

Some good advice I received recently was simple.

* Write the thoughts down.
* Give yourself a set amount of time to think about them.
* Let your mind go through them, including the negative thoughts, but set a boundary around that time.

I haven't mastered that skill yet, but it really resonates with me.

Because perhaps PMA isn't about trying to remove every negative thought.

Part of it is learning how to deal with them.

Never stop learning how your own mind works and how you can improve your mental processes and positivity, while still permitting yourself to consider the negative side.

And something I am sure I have said before in these posts:

Be kind to yourself. Give yourself some space.

I'm still learning.

What are you doing to improve yourself?

And what tips could you share that might help me or someone else reading this?
