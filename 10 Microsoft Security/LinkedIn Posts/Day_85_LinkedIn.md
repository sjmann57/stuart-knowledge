## LinkedIn Post (published — 2026-08-28)

Finding your groove is not always easy.

That statement is something I've heard more times than I can remember, whether I'm writing another article for my challenge, a client solution, a proposal, or solving a problem. What do you do?

That is a question that, when I am stuck and not sure how to move forward, when it feels as if I am walking through treacle with oversized shoes on. Do you stop and walk away?

Finding my groove this week has been difficult; I have had a couple of things on my plate that have given me that feeling. The task had a deadline, not one I could move, not something I could give away.

What do you do?

How do you get over that barrier?

Do you have a plan for it?

Can you turn to someone for help or inspiration?

All of these are good questions, and I am sure in your mind you are coming up with possible answers, reflecting on your process, even right now, and your distraction was scrolling through LinkedIn.

I have a few things I do to get over this and find my groove.

The first step for me is to walk away

Go for a walk and get another perspective for a short time. This helps when I've fallen out of the groove, and I am a bit blinded about what to do next. There is a risk here: instead of a reset, I run and hide, and I know I said that out loud. But I am sure you all know what I mean.

The second is to keep moving forward.

If it is writing a document, keep putting the framework and content together. Forcing myself to move forward beyond what I want to do can help me find the thread I need to get back into the groove and have my mojo back for the task at hand.

The third is to talk with someone who can give me another view.

Being asked questions like: how would you approach this, have you done this before, and get them to ask me questions.

This is by far my favourite approach, and it is one I use with other people, even when I have no idea about the subject or the challenges they're facing. Sometimes just being asked questions gives you an answer to a question that was never asked. You have a thread; you know what to do, you are back in the groove.

Not the final step, but one I used this week.

I get to a point where I either get document-blind and ask for a review. Normally, I ask for a review, which then refocuses me on my task; someone else will see what I can't; a comment might find the thread I was looking for; or the document or technical pitch was too complex or too simple.

That final review got me thinking, even before I got the feedback. My mind started to clear up what I needed to do, and I felt creative and excited about the task.

The funny part was that I was so engrossed in being back in the groove that I got on the wrong train home from London.

Well, first came the dread: the train left 15 minutes early, and I quickly checked the board and listened for announcements.

Thankfully, it was heading in the right direction, and although it wasn't stopping at my station, it would stop relatively nearby. So my son came, picked me up, and drove me 25 minutes back to my original station so I could pick up my car. I do have a good laugh about it.

My success this week is having ways to help myself.

So do you have a plan to get yourself back in the groove, to find your mojo for that task that feels like a weight around you?

I would really like to hear about your favourite action that helps you. It may help others who read this, so go on and share it with us all.
