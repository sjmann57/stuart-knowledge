## 1. Why This Topic Matters

Day 1 does one job: make the right people stop scrolling and think "I should talk to this person."

Most LinkedIn introduction posts fail because they read like a CV with line breaks. This one works differently — it opens with contrast and personality, establishes credibility through specificity rather than claims, and closes with a transparent statement of availability that feels confident rather than needy.

For recruiters and hiring managers, this post acts as an immediate signal: experienced, articulate, available, worth a call. For security peers and potential future clients, it establishes the foundation of a voice worth following.

Post this first. Everything else builds from it.

## 2. LinkedIn Post

*(Stuart's own words -- do not edit the substance, only format for LinkedIn if needed)*

---

I started working with Microsoft technology when the world was much less complex than it is today. Back then, the biggest threat was someone walking out with a floppy disk.

Since then, the threats have grown considerably more sophisticated. But the main challenge hasn't changed: people need their systems to work, and their data to stay protected. As someone who understands both the technical and commercial sides of making that happen.

Since my last career shift to cybersecurity over 8 years ago, I have worked as a Microsoft Security Architect. In that time, I have had some highly interesting projects and deliveries, from building SIEM solutions for MSSP to improving security for NHS trusts and financial services organisations.

My roles have spanned pre-sales conversations through to design and delivery, leading Sentinel deployments from scoping to detection engineering, and designing Zero Trust architecture for complex hybrid environments. They all have one thing in common: how they can improve and secure a business, not just by mitigating technical risks, but also by making it easy for the end user to carry out their normal job functions.

I hold a wide range of qualifications, including Microsoft SC-100, SC-200, SC-300, SC-400, and AZ-500, as well as ISC2 CCSP. Certifications matter less than what they represent: staying current in a fast-moving field. After leaving school, I thought, back then, my exam and qualification days were over; in reality, that was just the beginning; qualifications and learning never stop. I continue to attain new qualifications and explore what lies behind them.

What separates good security architecture from average security architecture is not always technical knowledge. It is understanding the organisation behind the technology, how it operates, and what it can not afford to lose. With a diverse career spanning multinational to smaller SMEs, I have gained a rounded view of security delivery and client engagement.

Now, with the world of AI upon us, the problems are still very much the same: what you allow and don't allow to happen to your data and systems is still critical.

So, what does your current Microsoft security challenge look like?

## 3. Suggested Image

**Option A — Professional headshot (strongest for Day 1)**
A clean, confident headshot. Natural light, neutral or blurred background, professional but not stiff. Not a passport photo. The kind of image that makes someone think "I'd trust this person in a board meeting." If you have one already that fits this description, use it. If not, this is worth investing in before you post Day 1.

**Option B — Text graphic (if no suitable photo)**
A dark background (deep navy or charcoal) with white text showing your name, title, and a single credential line:
> Stuart Mann
> Microsoft Security Architect
> SC-100 | SC-200 | SC-300 | AZ-500 | CCSP

Keep it minimal. No stock images, no clip art, no generic cyber graphics. Simple and clean is more credible than designed-on-a-Friday-afternoon.

**Recommendation:** Option A. Day 1 is the post people will see when they visit your profile after reading something you wrote. A face creates connection; a graphic doesn't.

## 4. Engagement Strategy

### Before you post — set the stage
- Update your LinkedIn headline to: *Microsoft Security Architect | SC-100 | Defender XDR · Sentinel · Entra ID | Available for Contract & Permanent Roles*
- Ensure your About section opens with 2–3 sentences about who you are and that you're actively looking
- Turn on Open to Work (Recruiters only or public — your preference)

### Immediately after posting
Send a personalised connection request to 10 recruiters active in Microsoft/cyber security. Don't reference the post — just connect with a short note:
> *"Hi [name] — I'm a Microsoft Security Architect, SC-100 certified, available now for contract or permanent. Happy to connect if it's useful to have me on your radar."*

### Communities to engage with today
- Posts tagged #MicrosoftSecurity and #CyberSecurity — comment on 3–5 with something substantive
- Any CISO or IT Director posting about security challenges — add your perspective
- Microsoft UK partner organisations active on LinkedIn — engage with their content

### Target audience for this post
Share it beyond your feed if possible:
- Tag 1–2 colleagues or former clients who know your work (ask permission first if unsure)
- Post in any LinkedIn Groups you're a member of that cover cyber security or Microsoft technology
