## LinkedIn Article (published — 2026-08-08)

Technical posts, personal posts… or both?

Over the past 65 days of writing on LinkedIn, I've noticed something interesting.

Some of my technical posts generate great conversations. Others, where I write more about the person behind the technology, receive a little less interaction. That got me thinking about what people really come to LinkedIn for.

What kind of platform do you see it as?

I've become far more active on LinkedIn. I've been writing, responding to messages and commenting on posts that genuinely interest me. LinkedIn even tells you how your posts and comments perform. I wonder if that's designed to make us all come back a little more often.

Writing four technical posts each week takes time. There is research, planning, fact-checking and trying to explain technical subjects in a way that people find useful. Limiting myself to four technical posts has helped me keep balance while continuing my 100-day challenge.

I love it when a technical subject resonates with people. The discussions in the comments, the direct messages and the different perspectives are often the best part of writing.

But then there are the posts where I talk about mindset, Scouting, training for an Ironman, family, leadership or the way I approach decisions. Somehow, they nearly always lead back to my professional mindset.

Those posts usually receive less interaction.
That's okay.

Because those posts are just as important to me.

The answer is simple.
> I'm not Microsoft Learn.
> I'm not an AI model.
> I'm not Google.
Those can all explain technology.

What they cannot explain is how I approach problems, make decisions, deal with setbacks, continue learning or try to help other people grow.

That's the part only I can write.

Everything I do influences everything else. The lessons I learn through Ironman training help me at work. My experiences in Scouting influence how I lead and support people. Conversations with clients shape how I think about architecture. Family keeps everything in perspective.

They're all connected.

If all I ever posted were technical articles, you would learn something about Microsoft Security.

By sharing some of my experiences as well, you'll learn something about the person writing them.

For those who enjoy seeing the person behind the technical posts, thank you. I genuinely appreciate every comment, conversation and message.
Don't worry, I won't be filling LinkedIn with holiday photo albums or pictures of what I had for dinner, but I will continue sharing the lessons, experiences and thought processes that have shaped me. I'll always try to bring those back to business, leadership, personal growth and continuous learning.

So I'm curious.

When you follow someone on LinkedIn, what keeps you coming back?
Technical expertise? Personal experiences? Leadership and mindset? Or a mix of them all?

And one final question.
If you could ask me, or anyone else you follow on LinkedIn, for one piece of advice, what question would you ask?
