## LinkedIn Post (Stuart's version — published)

What shapes your week?

Is it the emails you didn't read Friday, Teams messages still left unread, or do you have a better way?

For me, I am not always a good list person; I find it a bit of a challenge, but it works for many.

For me, a simple priority board works, splitting my tasks into 4 parts.

Although I don't follow this to a T, I do use my own version of the Eisenhower Matrix (Urgent/Important grid).

The Eisenhower Matrix is ancient but works because it's brutally honest. Instead of a flat to-do list, you sort everything into four quadrants:

**Do First (Urgent + Important)**
Client escalation. Security incident. Compliance audit deadline. This is where firefighting lives; handle it now or the week implodes.

**Schedule (Not Urgent + Important)**
Architecture review. Strategic project planning. Skill development. This is where real work happens, but it's easy to skip when urgent stuff drowns you out. Block time and defend it.

**Delegate (Urgent + Not Important)**
Meeting requests from people who aren't your stakeholders. Status updates. Admin tasks. Can someone else own this? If not, batch it and kill it fast.

**Drop (Not Urgent + Not Important)**
The noise. Nice-to-have side projects. Low-signal Slack threads. You're not actually going to do these, so stop pretending.

**The weekly audit:**
Spend 20 minutes mapping everything into the matrix. You'll cut 30% of your list immediately. The week gets sharper.

Where do you spend most of your time? And more importantly, where should you be spending it?
