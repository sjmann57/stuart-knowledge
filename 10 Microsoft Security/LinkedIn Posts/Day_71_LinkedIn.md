## LinkedIn Post (published — 2026-08-14)

Success Friday: "I don't know" can be a good answer

How is that success, you may ask?

When I first started my career, I was in the service industry, but not in the way I see it today.

I wore a suit and tie every day and took three buses to work, getting there well before 9am so I could make everyone tea and coffee.

Then I got on with fixing and cleaning typewriters, counting ribbon stock, helping the other engineers and doing anything else that needed doing.

There was no Google. No mobile phones. No tap-and-go anything. It was all cash, and I kept much of what I needed in my head.

Looking back, I think there was an advantage to starting my career in that generation.

That experience taught me so much. I had to ask lots of questions, be present in the moment and stay focused.

For someone who can easily be distracted and is always curious about the next shiny thing, it was probably a good time in history for me to start my career.

Technology moved very quickly from that point.

Computers arrived. Then we networked them together. Eventually, we moved into the cloud.

Jobs changed. Businesses changed. My career changed with them.

Technology started moving quicker and quicker, and it hasn't slowed down since.

When I talk to my peers, many of whom I now call friends, we all feel the same way about the rate of change.

So, why is "I don't know" a good thing?

I am not an encyclopaedia, a Google search or an AI LLM.

I am human.

My memory can be affected by how much sleep I've had, how hungry I am and simply how I feel that day.

Sometimes I cannot remember every answer.

And "I don't know" is a good answer.

There is a caveat.

We should accept that we won't know every answer and be comfortable saying, "I don't know.", but I can find out.

And that, for me, is the key.

Success isn't knowing every answer.

Success is knowing where to find the answers, understanding how to interpret what you find, and knowing how to bring a good solution to the table.

So take some of that pressure off yourself.

In my experience, you think better that way.

What is your success that keeps on giving back?

(Yes, that is a very young me in my 80's suit and much-loved paisley tie)
