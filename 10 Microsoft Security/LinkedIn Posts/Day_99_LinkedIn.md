## LinkedIn Article (final — ready to post, 2026-09-11)

Day 99 of 100 days of writing: the body of work and what it built

Today, on day 99 of my 100-day writing challenge, the finish line is approaching, and I want to step back and look at what I actually built.

The idea wasn't mine; I cannot take credit for 100 posts in 100 days. My inspiration was Peter Rising. When I started, the commitment was simple: write, share what I know, and keep turning up. I needed to show who I am and the value I bring.

Looking back through the posts, I can see how much ground I have covered. Microsoft security, career changes, confidence, Scouting, Ironman training and the people who support me.

Together, those posts say something about what I know and how I approach life and work.

What got built

Across the challenge, I have written about a broad range of Microsoft Security topics, covering a broad range of Microsoft security subjects:

* Identity and access: Privileged Identity Management, Conditional Access, Identity Protection, authentication strengths, Entra ID Governance Lifecycle Workflows and Workload ID.
* Threat protection: Defender for Endpoint, Office 365, Cloud Apps, Identity, Vulnerability Management and Defender XDR.
* Cloud security: Defender for Cloud, Servers, Containers and DevOps security.
* Data security: Purview DLP, sensitivity labels, Data Security Posture Management, Insider Risk Management and Communication Compliance.
* Access and security operations: Global Secure Access, Microsoft Sentinel, Security Exposure Management and Security Copilot.
* Zero Trust: Principles, assessment, identity and putting the findings into practice.

I also wrote a four-part series on securing AI agents, covering Microsoft Agent 365, Entra Agent ID, Purview for agent data and Microsoft Defender threat protection. These are connected parts of Microsoft's approach to securing AI agents.

I used Microsoft documentation, including Microsoft Learn, to research the technical pieces, alongside my own experience.

The question running through much of the writing was practical: you have the licence and the product, but what have you configured, and what is it doing for you?

That gave the collection a thread: individual articles, connected by the work of turning security capabilities into something useful.

It is a body of work I am proud of, and I hope it has helped others get to understand security better.

The numbers, honestly

I have recorded figures along the way, although the record is incomplete.

On 13 August, my tracker totalled 28,900 impressions across 38 posts. That is a dated snapshot. Some posts have later figures, and others still have no recorded metrics.

Among the technical posts, the recorded figures include:

* Microsoft Entra Workload ID: 5,633 impressions, recorded on 27 August.
* Microsoft Agent 365: 2,684 impressions, recorded on 15 August.
* Purview sensitivity labels: 2,199 impressions, recorded on 27 August.
* Posts on DLP in simulation mode, Defender for Office 365, and Purview for AI agent data also passed 1,000 recorded impressions.
* And the personal writing deserves its place here. My job-search retrospective recorded 2,965 impressions by 13 August.

Those figures show that different subjects found an audience. They do not tell me exactly why someone stopped scrolling, or what they took away.

The comments and messages gave me more of that picture.

What changed along the way

By day 30, I had accepted a job offer. On day 40, I shared that I was joining CyberOne as Senior Microsoft 365 Security Consultant.

I see the writing as part of that wider effort.

I also worked on my CV, updated my LinkedIn profile, spoke with recruiters, asked for introductions, and prepared for interviews. Each conversation helped me think about the next one.

The writing gave me a place to explain how I think about Microsoft security and share some of the experience behind my CV.

Finding the right role mattered. So did finding people and a culture I wanted to work with.

By day 50, I was writing about enjoying the new role, making connections, having conversations and improving how I presented my message.

Those were things worth celebrating while the challenge was still going.

The conversations mattered

One outcome that stays with me comes from day 64. People were sending messages about careers, learning, confidence and decisions they were trying to make. Those conversations are still continuing, and I have hopefully inspired others.

Being trusted with those questions meant something special to me. Helping others is in my core.

I rarely tell someone what they should do. I explain how I approach a decision and ask questions that might help them think through their own situation.

Their circumstances are different from mine. Their priorities might be different too.

Sometimes a useful question helps someone find their own next step.

Those conversations became part of the value of writing. They gave me something to think about, too.

A foundation for what comes next

I am collecting the technical work as evidence towards my ambition to become a Microsoft MVP in Security. I have also started representing CyberOne publicly, and my first webinar in a while yesterday on AI.

That is a goal I am working towards, and it will need more than just writing LinkedIn articles.

The articles give me something concrete to build on: subjects I have researched, explanations I have worked through and contributions I can point to.

The process has helped me learn too.

Explaining a subject makes me slow down. I have to think about what the reader needs to know, what I am assuming and where I need to check my understanding.

That has value in my day-to-day work.

The work behind the posts

* By day 18, I had already admitted that posting regularly was harder than I expected.
* By day 50, I was writing about long days, still finishing a post at 9 pm.
* On day 72, I sat at the kitchen table wondering what I could write about. That became the post. I stayed there until it was done.

Those moments belong in this story too.

The collection includes research, rewriting, blank pages and days when I needed to ask for help.

It also includes the parts of my life that influence how I work: Scouting, endurance sport, family, friendships and the people I have learnt from.

I wanted you to see the person writing the technical articles.

Thank you

To everyone who read, reacted, commented, shared or sent me a message: thank you.

Thank you for the questions and the different perspectives. They gave me reasons to think again, explain something better or look at a subject from another angle.

And thank you to Jeanie Mann, my family, and the friends who have been in my corner and have been key to my last 100 days.

I have written before about how much it helps to have someone who listens and asks the question that gets your thinking moving again. I have been fortunate to have those people around me.

As the challenge nears its end, I have a collection of writing I can build on, conversations I value, and a clearer sense of what I want to keep sharing.

I am glad I kept going.
