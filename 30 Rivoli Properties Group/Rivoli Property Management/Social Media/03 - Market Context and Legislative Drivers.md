# Market Context and Legislative Drivers — Why Now

Two live regulatory threads anchor RPM's "why switch" and "why Rivoli" content pillars. Any legislative claim used in a post must be sourced and dated — this is the one content category where getting it wrong is a real liability, not just a brand-voice miss.

## 1. The Renters' Rights Act 2025

In force since **1 May 2026**. It removed Section 21 ("no-fault") evictions and fixed-term ASTs for traditional lets, moving all standard tenancies to periodic terms. Serviced accommodation generally sits outside this regime because it is not let as someone's main home under an assured tenancy.

This is already a documented driver of landlords switching from traditional letting to SA in 2026.

Source: [TeamStay — The Renters' Rights Act 2026: Why Traditional Landlords Are Switching to Serviced Accommodation](https://teamstay.co.uk/teamstay-for-providers/the-renters-rights-act-2026-why-traditional-landlords-are-switching-to-serviced-accommodation/) · [Wikipedia — Renters' Rights Act 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renters%27_Rights_Act_2025)

## 2. National short-term-let registration scheme

The UK government is progressing a mandatory national registration scheme for short-term lets in England, expected to begin in 2026 — meaning the regulatory bar for running compliant SA is rising.

This cuts two ways for content: it's a source of landlord anxiety ("am I compliant?") and a trust signal for Rivoli Management ("we already handle this for you").

Sources: [myshortlets.com — Short-Term Let Rules in the UK: What to Expect by 2026](https://myshortlets.com/new-short-term-let-rules-in-the-uk-2026/) · [GOV.UK — Letting out a self-catering holiday home in England: rules and regulations](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/letting-out-a-self-catering-holiday-home-in-england-rules-and-regulations)
